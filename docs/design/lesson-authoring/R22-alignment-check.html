Learning in Action

30 min

Part A: Reading the opening with pause points RL.6.1L.6.418 min

Teacher tip
The narrator mentions a learning difference and being called a troubled kid. Keep the talk on how the narrator sees himself; don’t ask students to share their own labels.
Teacher
Read pages 1–8 aloud while students follow along. Use your voice to show the narrator’s sarcastic asides so students hear the tone.

Partner Reading & Discussion

Directions
As I read, mark any sentence where the narrator tells you how he feels about himself. Put a star next to the one that surprises you most.
Ask
Pause after page 3. The narrator warns readers to stop reading. What does that warning suggest about how he sees his own life?
Exemplar
It suggests he thinks his life is dangerous or strange, and he doesn’t want normal kids to end up like him.
Ask
Pause after page 6. The root mort means death. What does immortal mean, and why might it matter in this story?
Exemplar
Immortal means not able to die. It might matter because it hints that the myths could be real in this book.
Pulse check (RL.6.1, L.6.4)
The narrator is on probation at school. Which meaning of probation fits best?
  • A
    A school award for good behavior
    Incorrect: A student may pick this if they read the word without the context of being in trouble.
  • B
    A trial period when behavior is watched before a final decision
    Correct: It fits a student who has been in trouble and is being given one more chance.
  • C
    A test given at the end of the school year
    Incorrect: A student may pick this because both happen at school; nothing in the text points to a test.
  • D
    A break from classes for a family trip
    Incorrect: A student may confuse it with time away from school; probation means staying and being watched.

Part B: Writing about the narrator’s fears RL.6.1RL.6.212 min

Directions
Write three to four sentences that answer the question. Use one detail from today’s pages and the words “This suggests that.”
Ask
How do Percy’s comments in the opening pages show his world values (and fears) about “normal” kids, and how might these opening pages be setting him up to become a hero or “problem” figure in a modern myth?
Exemplar
Percy tells readers they should stop reading if they think they are like him. This suggests that he is afraid of being different and wishes he were a normal kid. He also jokes about the schools he has been kicked out of, which suggests that adults have already decided he is a problem. These pages could be setting him up to be a hero who proves everyone wrong.
Reflection (RL.6.1)
How sure are you that your detail supports your inference? Rate yourself from 1 to 5 using the Reflection routine.

Look Back

5 min

Quick Write

Directions
In two sentences, predict what kind of hero Percy might become. Use one word from today’s vocabulary.
Ask
What is one thing Percy fears, and how might that fear matter later in a myth-like story?
Exemplar
Percy fears being a problem kid forever. In a myth the hero usually has to face what they fear, so he might have to prove he belongs.

Homework

Teacher
Have students take the novel home. Instruct students to:
  • Read and annotate Chapter 1, pp. 9–15. Mark two places where Percy’s tone changes.
  • Read and annotate the excerpt from “The Hare and the Lion.”
